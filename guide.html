Mapping the Dark Universe

About 85% of all matter gives off no light at all. You can still map it, by watching how it bends light from a hundred million distant galaxies. This tour walks you from that idea to the team's own measurement.

    Where cosmology stands

    How clumpy is the universe? (S8)

    The CMB "baby photo" predicts today's clumpiness; lensing surveys weigh it directly. Dots are best values, bars the 68% range.

    Shaded band: Planck 2018 ± 1σ. Lower means a smoother universe. Sources at the bottom of the page.

    Clumpiness (S8) tension

    narrowing · survey-dependent

    Lensing once found the universe a few percent smoother than Planck predicts. KiDS-Legacy (2025) now agrees with Planck; DES Y6 (2026) still sits 2.6σ low on S8. A 2026 review calls it probe-dependent and unresolved.

    Expansion-rate (H0) tension

    unresolved · ~5σ

    Nearby stars say 73.0 ± 1.0 km/s/Mpc (SH0ES); the early universe implies 67.4 ± 0.5. JWST red-giant distances (CCHP) land between, at 70.4. Experts disagree on the cause.

    Is dark energy changing?

    hint · 2.8–4.2σ

    DESI's 3D galaxy map plus the CMB and supernovae prefers dark energy that weakens over time, at 2.8–4.2σ depending on the supernova set. That is short of the 5σ discovery bar, and newer DESI Lyman-alpha results lean back toward a constant.

    Bigger maps are coming

    underway

    Rubin Observatory started its 10-year sky survey on 29 June 2026. Euclid releases its first raw data and calibrated images in November 2026, with full science products in mid-2027.
